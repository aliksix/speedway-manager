DEFINITION
FIM Europe organises European Championships and Prize Events (Cups,
Trophies etc.). The FIM Europe Championships and Prize Events are staged as a
series of qualifying events leading up to a Final / Final Series.
1 LICENSES
1.1 Riders
Licenses for all European Championships and Prize events are issued by the FIM
Europe or FIM.
The minimum obligatory insurance coverage for riders and passengers is as
follows:
- For medical treatment - 14.000 Euro
- For repatriation - 3.400 Euro

1.3 Entrants
An entry form for each competitor (nominated by his/her FMN to compete in a FIM
Europe Individual Championship or Cup event) must be completed by the rider
before each event NO LATER than fifteen days before the event using the
official Track Racing Commission entry form ­ which is located within the
FIME `on-line' entry form system. Once completed, the entry form must then
be approved by the riders' FMN, before finally being submitted to the FIME
Track Racing Commission.
This entry form will confirm that the rider (and passenger if applicable), is aware
that he/she must compete in all rounds of the respective championship or Cup ­
should they qualify.
The SEC Finals are an exception to this rule and rule 12.9.6 will apply.
In the situation of a rider nomination being announced later than this deadline date,
the announcement must be made immediately, in order that the rider can prepare
for the event and send his completed entry form as above without further delay.
For `Team'/Pairs events, entry forms must be completed by the appointed Team
Manager using the official Track Racing Commission entry form ­ which is located
within the FIME `on-line' entry form system and submitted no later than fifteen
days prior to each event (Semi Final, Qualifying Round and Final).
Once the entry form has been submitted, the rider(s) must participate in the event,
unless medically unfit ­ which must be confirmed by a Medical Certificate. In this
situation the FMN of the rider can nominate a replacement rider to the meeting (if
it applies). In Team/Pairs events, this rider must be entered with the starting
number of the rider he replaces.
If any members of the team are not present until the commencement of the first
heat, the team reserve rider must replace him with his starting number.
A competitor or a team who refuses a nomination by their FMN to compete
in an FIM Europe Championship or Cup event or, having accepted
nomination refuses to take part, shall be deemed banned internationally for
a minimum period of ONE day before to THREE days after the meeting
concerned.
Furthermore, the competitor or a team shall be considered ineligible for all FIM
Europe championship events for the remainder of the season.
A competitor, having been declared and medically certified as unfit to take part in
an event (to the satisfaction of either the FIME or the Jury), shall be banned for a
minimum period of one day before (where applicable) to THREE days after the
event concerned.

A competitor or a team who starts in a FIME Championship or Cup and
qualifies for the next round (i.e. Semi-Final, Final or Final Series) must
participate unless prevented from doing so by injury or illness (certified in
writing to that effect by a suitably qualified Doctor) or other "force majeure".
A decision on the acceptability of other "force majeure" must be taken by
the International Jury of the meeting from which the rider is absent or by the
FIM Europe Track Racing Commission, if the absence is known before the
race meeting.
If the race meeting Doctor/CMO declares the rider unfit, the rider cannot continue
racing in that event. The rider will also be banned for the period of time
recommended to the International Jury by the Doctor/CMO of the race meeting. In
this case, the rider must undergo a medical examination before the start of the
next event.
A rider refusing to continue racing in an FIME event will be banned for a period
decided by the International Jury of the race meeting. In this situation the
International Jury must also consider any possible "force majeure".
When a rider is withdrawing for any reasons and the withdrawal is accepted by the
International Jury, the rider is not allowed to re-enter that event.
In all Championship and Cup events, where the age of participants must be
verified by the Jury, a passport or a formal identity should be presented for this
purpose. In team competitions, all members of a team must present the passport
or a formal identity of the country they represent.
During an FIME event, the Jury, Referee or appointed Race Director has the
authority to penalise an FIME licensed rider, a FIM/FIME licensed Official or any
other person (e.g. volunteers), in line with the FIME Disciplinary Code. Refer also
to section 29 `Fines'.
1.4 Substitute Riders
For all events of any FIME competition (except Team events), at least two
substitute riders/Sidecars teams must be available.
For the first round of any Championship/Cup, the substitute riders/Sidecars teams
are nominated by the FMNR.
For the following rounds of any competition, the substitute riders will be qualifiers
from the previous rounds as stipulated in the rules for relevant Championship/Cup
events.
If the rules do not provide another solution, the substitute who has qualified from
the 1st Semi Final (or Qualifying Round) will be the first substitute on the Starting
List of the Final, then the first substitute from the 2nd Semi Final (or Qualifying
Round) and so on.
Prior to publishing an event Starting List ­ in the situation of an injured/unavailable
rider(s) ­ then he/they will be replaced by the next rider(s) from the same Semi
Final (or Qualifying Round) ­ should time allow.
Once the Starting List has been published, an injured/unavailable rider will be
replaced by the first substitute on the Starting List and so on. The substitute places
on the Starting List will then be completed by riders nominated the FMNR as Track
Reserves.
Also in order to complete the line-up of competitors in the case of a non-
appearance of the nominated/qualified riders and substitutes in any event, the

FMNR can nominate their own riders/Sidecar team as Track Reserves.
The points scored by a Track Reserve count towards the result of the event he is
competing in (so the rider/Sidecar team is classified in the overall result of this
event), unless the Track Reserve participated in any previous round of the
competition and has not qualified further.
1.5 Tracks
Tracks used for European Championships or Cups must be homologated by the
FIM Europe or the FIM and have a valid FIM Europe or FIM track license.
It is now compulsory that during all FIME Track Racing events, a large format
scoreboard (e.g. printed on thick card, `Correx' board or similar material, or a large
TV screen), should be positioned in an easily accessible place within the pits. The
Clerk of the Course must appoint a designed person whose responsibility is to
update the scoreboard after each heat, in line with the official results as
announced by the Referee.
A promotional car/vehicle (specifically used for advertising purposes) or other
similar marketing device (maximum dimensions: 5 mtrs in length x 3 mtrs wide x
2 mtrs high), may be placed on the centre green of the track during racing (as
close to the middle as possible), only if the organiser/promoter has suitable and
specific insurance which covers such a situation.
In addition, suitable arrangements must be in place for the protection of the
vehicle/object (by the use of thick foam or air bags positioned on all
corners/sides), and these arrangements must have been accepted by the Jury.
1.6 Centre Green Personnel
Due to a number of recent `near miss incidents', it is strongly recommended that
only those staff necessary should be positioned within the centre green (e.g. Clerk
of Course, flag marshals, appointed photographers [where applicable] and First
Aid personnel).
It is STRONGLY RECOMMENDED that if `Grid Girls' form part of the pre-race
presentation, then once the riders arrive at the tapes, the Grid Girls must then
leave the track and stand behind the safety fence and neutral zone.

2.2 Appeals against the Jury
Appeals against the decision of the Jury or the Referee of the meeting have to be
made to the FIM CDI (Disciplinary Commission) observing the procedure stated
by the Disciplinary and Arbitration Code of the FIM Europe.

4 SUPPLEMENTARY REGULATIONS
The Supplementary Regulations (SR's) for an event must be sent directly to the
FIM Europe Track Racing Commission (TRC) Chairman for approval AT LEAST
60 DAYS prior to the event. The TRC Chairman will signify approval of the
Supplementary Regulations by adding his signature to the last page of the SR's.
Once the SR's have been approved, the TRC Chairman will send a copy to the
FMNR, the Jury President/ Referee and the FIM Europe General Secretariat.
In the situation where the TRC Chairman does not receive the Supplementary
Regulations within the specified time frame of 60 days, the FMNR responsible for
the meeting will be fined 130 
The organiser of a European Championship or Cup event must provide the Jury
President with a copy of the insurance policy written in English or French covering
their own third-party liability and those of the manufacturers, riders, passengers,
sponsors and officials in case of accidents to third parties during an event or during
any practice sessions.
The insurance policy shall also cover any possible liability of the FIM Europe to
third parties.
The minimum amount of coverage of the third-party liability insurance provided for
is 350.000  per event.
Note 1: The third-party insurance will come into effect one day before the official
practice session and will terminate two days after the last race day.
Note 2: A copy of the third-party liability insurance document must be emailed to
the TRC NO LATER than seven days before the meeting.
It is now compulsory for the organiser of any FIME Track Racing event (except
85cc/190cc and 125cc) to ensure that a supply of Methanol (together with a
suitable certificate) is available at the stadium/track (either supplied free of charge
or against payment ­ as per the applicable regulations).
If the methanol is chargeable, then information confirming the price must be
stipulated in the event Supplementary Regulations.

12 INDIVIDUAL SPEEDWAY EURO CHAMPIONSHIP (SEC)
12.1 General
The Individual Speedway Euro Championship (SEC) consists of the `Qualification
stage' with three Qualifying Rounds, the Individual Speedway Euro Championship
Challenge (ECC) and the series of four `Final' events.
The Final series will be organised in partnership with a contractual FIM Europe
Promoter.
The Individual Speedway Euro Championship Bureau (ECB) is a specific
commission responsible for controlling the organisation of the Individual
Speedway Euro Championship Final series. The ECB will consist of four
members ­ two members from the contractual FIM Europe Promoter and two
members from FIM Europe Track Racing Commission. The ECB Chairman will be
the Chairman of FIM Europe Track Racing Commission. Decisions of the ECB are
undertaken by voting and require simple majority in votes. In case of a tie, the
Chairman will exercise a casting vote.
12.2 Participants
For the `Qualification stage' of this championship, each FMN can nominate their
own riders. The riders will then be allocated (by the FIM Europe TRC) to the
various Qualifying Round events. The FMNR of each Qualifying Round can
nominate two substitutes to this event. The line-up of riders for the Final series
events will be completed by nominated and Wild Card riders as stipulated in 12.9
Note: FIM Speedway Grand Prix `permanent' riders are not entitled to participate
in the SEC Qualifying Rounds or the ECC.

18 EUROPEAN INDIVIDUAL GRASS TRACK CHAMPIONSHIP
18.1 General
18.1.1 Event Format
The competition is for Individual riders and will consist of a Grass Track
`Challenge' and one `Final' event.
All events will consist of eighteen riders, (plus two Track Reserves).
An organiser may apply to the FIME Track Racing Commission (FIME
TRC), to stage a round of the European Individual and Sidecar
Championship on the same day (i.e. a `double event').
However if an organiser applies to stage a `double event', then they will
NOT be able to incorporate any National/additional heats into the
meeting.
18.1.2 Additional Protective Devices (APD's) and Track Safety Fencing
Tracks used for FIME Grass Track championship events, must comply with
the current FIM Standards for Track Racing (STRC).
From 2026 the use of FIM homologated APD's (air type fences), is
compulsory at all FIME Grass Track and Sidecar Racing championship
events.
In addition `flexible sections' of the safety fence must be provided on
the exit of bends 2 and 4. These requirements must also be provided for
FIME race meetings held on `temporary tracks'
18.1.3 Support/Additional Races
In an FIME championship event where additional `Support/Additional'
heats are incorporated; a maximum of ten additional heats would be
acceptable for a non-televised event.
When submitting the draft SR's to the TRC for approval, the organiser
must also submit a draft race schedule ­ containing a list of all planned
races (including any additional heats) for the event. This list will be
reviewed and confirmed by the TRC Chairman although it may be
subject to alteration.
However if the event is being filmed/televised, then NO additional heats
may be staged during the European Grass Track Championship event.
Nevertheless, additional heats may be staged following the completion
of the European Championship races and the winner's prize giving
ceremony. In this situation, the draft race schedule (referred to in the
preceding paragraph), must show that the additional heats will be
staged following the completion of the European Championship event
and the winner's prize giving ceremony.
If due to circumstances on the day of the event, the programme needs
to be condensed, then the International Jury may take the decision to
reduce or delete some or all of the additional heats.
18.1.4 Riding/Start Numbers
Riders competing in the European Individual Grass Track
Championship, may apply to use their own `personal' racing number on
their race suit/race jacket and motorcycle.

19 EUROPEAN SIDECAR CHAMPIONSHIP
19.1 General
19.1.1 Event Format
The competition is for Sidecar teams and will consist of a Sidecar
`Challenge' and one `Final' event.
Both events will consist of twelve Sidecar teams (or outfits) plus two Track
Reserve Sidecar teams who will compete in each event.
An organiser may apply to the FIME Track Racing Commission (FIME
TRC), to stage a round of the European Sidecar and Individual
Championship on the same day (i.e. a `double event').
However if an organiser applies to stage a `double event', then they will
NOT be able to incorporate any National/additional heats into the
meeting.
19.1.2 Definition of a Team
The Sidecar rider and passenger enter the championships as a `team' and
should participate together throughout all rounds of the competition.
A change of passenger (e.g. someone different to the original passenger
qualifying into the Final), can only be approved by the TRC Chairman (or his
Deputy), in exceptional circumstances (e.g. injury, retirement from the
sport or similar exceptional situations).
Once such permission has been given, the original passenger cannot
participate in any FIME Sidecar championship event for the rest of the
season.
Note: The Sidecar rider is the nominated representative of the Sidecar team.
Although the rider will be nominated by his/her respective federation, and
would hold a national licence of that federation, his/her passport may be from
a different country (e.g. a German Sidecar rider holding a Dutch licence and
nominated to the European championships by the KNMV).
In this example, if the rider was to win either the Sidecar `Challenge', or the
European Final, then the GERMAN national anthem should be played during the
winner's prize giving ceremony (e.g. the national anthem of his/her country of origin).
19.1.3 Technical Specification
For further information regarding the technical specification for 500cc
Sidecar's refer to 34. Appendix 1 at the end of this document.
The use of rev limiters (as stipulated within the FIM Track Racing Technical
rules for 500cc engines) is compulsory.
19.1.4 Additional Protective Devices (APD's) and Track Safety Fencing
Tracks used for FIME Grass Track championship events, must comply with
the current FIM Standards for Track Racing (STRC).
From 2026 the use of FIM homologated APD's (air type fences), is
compulsory at all FIME Grass Track and Sidecar Racing championship
events.

the exit of bends 2 and 4. These requirements must also be provided

19.1.5 Support/Additional Races
In an FIME championship event where additional `Support/Additional heats
are incorporated a maximum of ten support races would be acceptable
for a non-televised event.
When submitting the draft SR's to the TRC for approval, the organiser
must also submit a draft race schedule ­ containing a list of all planned
races (including any additional heats) for the event. This list will be
reviewed and confirmed by the TRC Chairman although it may be
subject to alteration.
However if the event is being filmed/televised, then NO additional heats
may be staged during the European Sidecar Championship event.
Nevertheless, additional heats may be staged following the completion
of the European Championship races and the winner's prize giving
ceremony. In this situation, the draft race schedule (referred to in the
preceding paragraph), must show that the additional heats will be
staged following the completion of the European Sidecar
Championship event and the winner's prize giving ceremony.
If due to circumstances on the day of the event, the programme needs
to be condensed, then the International Jury may take the decision to
reduce or delete some or all of the additional heats.
19.1.6 Riding/Start Numbers
Riders competing in the European Sidecar Championship, may apply
to use their own `personal' racing number on their race suit/race jacket
and motorcycle.
They may choose any riding number with a maximum of three digits,
provided that it has not been previously allocated to another rider ­ and
the number does not start with a 0 (zero). Riding numbers 1 to 14 are
reserved for the use of FIME only.
To request a specific `personal riding number', the Sidecar team must
apply to the FIME TRC when they submit their entry form for a European
Sidecar Championship event.
Should more than one Sidecar team request the same `personal racing
number', preference will be given to the team who has a history of using
this number regularly in previous seasons or at a number of previous
events. If the situation can still not be agreed, then the riding number
will be allocated to the Sidecar team with the higher-ranking position in
the previous year's European championship results.
Sidecar teams who have not requested a `personal' riding number
within the deadline will be issued one by the TRC.
In each European Sidecar Championship event, riding numbers 13 and
14 will be allocated to the Track Reserve riders.

positioning and minimum sizes of both the national flag and riding/start
number.
Both the rider and passenger must wear the allocated helmet colour
when competing in a race.
19.2 Practice
19.2.1 One Day Event
It is strongly recommended that when an event is being staged on a Grass
Track circuit, in addition to the practice session allocated to riders
competing in any `additional heats' a maximum of four support races
(if approved in advance as in Art. 19.1.5.) may be held PRIOR to the
commencement of the FIME championship practice.
NOTE: this requirement is requested to ensure that the `green' grass is
removed from the race circuit prior to the staging of the FIM Europe
Sidecar practice, but without damaging the track surface.
If practice is planned on the same day as the event, then it must be scheduled
a minimum of three hours prior to the first heat.
In order to organise practice in line with the FIME practice schedule, it is
strongly recommended that the club/organiser allows one and a half hours for
the practice session to be staged. This will ensure Sidecar teams have
sufficient time to make any adjustments between their scheduled practice
sessions, as well as allowing sufficient time for any track
grading/maintenance that may be required.

practice followed by two laps must be provided.

26.9 Starts
Engines must be kept running, machines parallel with the inner edge of the
track and front wheels not more than 10 cm from each row starting line.
The Referee ­ when satisfied that the riders are correctly positioned ­
must switch on the green light indicating that the start is imminent. After
a pause long enough for the riders to fix their attention on the green light
and are standing still, the Referee must switch off the green light that
permitted the start.
26.9.1 False Starts
Any competitor who allows their motorcycle to move forward after the
Referee has switched on the green light must start again from the Penalty
Lane.
26.9.2 Penalty Lane
The Penalty Lane is positioned 15 metres back from the start/finish line
and marked with a single line. A rider can be positioned in the Penalty
Lane at the sole discretion of the Referee.
The first penalised rider will start on the outside line of the track (closest
to the safety fence if one is in place).
If more riders are intended to start from the Penalty Lane, then they will
be positioned in the chronological order they have been penalised,
starting from the outside to the inside line of the track.

26.11 Dangerous Riding
The Referee will immediately penalise any rider/riders, whom he considers
indulges in foul, unfair or dangerous riding.
A rider, who, having started in a heat does not make an honest attempt to
ride to the best of their ability, as judged by the Referee, will be guilty of an
offence, which can entail disqualification from the heat or a penalty.
The penalty consists of the rider starting from the Penalty Lane ­ should
there be a re-run.
There will be no protest or appeal against a Referee's decision to declare
a heat completed or as to the Referee's statement of foul, unfair or
dangerous riding.
If, in the opinion of the Referee, such conduct produces an advantage to
the rider involved or affects the chances of one or more riders, the Referee
will stop the heat and order a re-run.
If any rider is unable to cross the finish line as a result of foul, unfair or
dangerous riding on the part of another rider who, in consequence, has
been disqualified, the disadvantaged rider will be deemed to have finished
the heat in the placing held immediately before the foul, unfair or
dangerous riding incident and allowing for any advancement in placing
following the disqualification of the guilty rider.
A similar system will apply for a rider who ­ in the opinion of the Referee
­ has deliberately laid down his motorcycle or has left the course in the
interest of safety.
26.12 Re-staging or Abandonment of a Race Meeting
If a race is interrupted or suspended for any reason whatsoever, the
following rules will apply:
 Interrupted before Heat 9 is accomplished and the remaining heats
cannot be completed the same day, then the results are not taken
into consideration.
 Interrupted before Heat 12 is accomplished and the remaining heats
cannot be completed, then the race points scored at the completion
of Heat 9 will determine the final race meeting classification.
 Interrupted before the Last Chance heat is accomplished and the
Final cannot be completed, then therider's total race points scored
at the completion of Heat 12 will determine the final race meeting
classification.
In the event of a tie in the points scored at the completion of Heat 9 or
12, the finishing order will be determined by the formula set out in article
26.15.
If the meeting is abandoned before the Final heat is accomplished and the
remaining races cannot be completed, then the riders placed 7th - 14th will
have already been determined from the Last Chance heat. 1st - 6th and 15th
to 24th will be determined by the intermediate classification after Heat 12.

26.13 Starting Assistance
After the green light has been switched on (or other starting warning given),
no outside assistance can be given to any rider.
If, at that time, a rider's machine is not moving under its own power, the
rider concerned will start from the Penalty Lane.
26.14 Re-Run Race
If an accident occurs on the track and, in the opinion of the Referee, it is
dangerous for the race to continue, the Referee must stop the race. Only
the Referee is empowered to order a race to be stopped.
Any rider who, for any reason, is deemed to have been the primary cause
of a race being stopped shall be disqualified or penalised.
The Referee may permit any rider who has fallen ­ because of having been
fouled or because they have deliberately laid down their motorcycle or left
the course in the interest of safety to take part in the re-run. In such cases
any outside assistance may be ignored.
Any rider eligible to take part in a re-run can change their motorcycle for
the re-run.
When taking part in a re-run, riders must start from their original starting
positions except for the penalised rider(s).
Any rider who has failed to start in, has retired from (which includes not
proceeding under power when the heat was stopped) or has been
disqualified from a heat which has been ordered to be re-run, will be
ineligible to take part in the re-run, except in circumstances where they
have retired after being fouled or have stopped in the interest of safety as
described above.
26.15 Results
26.15.1 Calculation of Results
Results are determined by points relating to finishing order, or in the
Last Chance and the Final (without points) which are determined only
relating to the finishing order and chronological order in case of riders
not finishing a heat.
The order of the Final heat will be the order of final classification from
1st to 8th place.
The riders from 9th to 14th place are placed according to the order of
crossing the finishing line in the Last Chance heat.
Positions 15th to 24th are according to the Intermediate Classification
following the qualifying heats.

26.15.2 Ties
After the completion of the qualifying heats, and when establishing the
order between the riders in the `Intermediate Classification', any ties will
be solved as follows:
a) Precedence will be given to the riders (tied on race points) having
the better placings (a "0" for last placing is better than being
disqualified for whatever reason).
b) If the tie persists, precedence will be given to the better placed rider
in the heat or heats where the 2 riders have met.
c) If solutions a) or b) cannot be used, the order will be determined by
a ballot.

27.1 Individual Speedway, Ice Speedway, 85cc/190cc Youth Speedway, 250cc
Youth Speedway and 125cc Youth Track Racing Cup.
In all Championship and Cup events, after the official programme of heats has

between riders:
- Should there be a tie for the first place (this does not apply to formulas
where the Final Heat /Heats decides the overall classification of a
meeting, e.g. SEC Finals) OR for the last qualifying place or substitute
position, to go forward to the next round or for the first three places in
the overall classification of a Championship or Cup, there will be an
additional heat (run-off) to resolve the tie.
Starting gate positions for the run-off will be chosen in the presence of the Clerk
of the Course and either the Referee and/or the Jury President (if nominated).
When establishing the finishing order between the riders in the list of
intermediate or final results of all events (applicable also for abandoned events
with run-offs not included), ties will be solved as follows:
a) Precedence will be given to the riders, tied on points, having the greatest
number of 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th placing (an "0" for last placing being better
than M-R-F-T-d-N).
b) If the tie persists, providing riders have met equal times, and involves:
· Two riders ­ precedence to the best placed when the two riders
met. If still tied as both a non-finisher when they met, move to
point c).
· Three riders ­ if one rider beat the other two, they are deemed
best placed, and the best placed when the other two met is
deemed the higher of these two.
If the riders are still tied as no precedence as described, or non-
finishes when met ­ move to point c).
· Four or more riders tied ­ the `who beat who' principle within the
group of tied riders, totalling wins v loses when the riders met.
If the riders are still tied with the number of wins within the group,
they can then be split into `sub groups' and use the same
procedure above for splitting two or three riders if necessary.
If all four or more riders are still tied, then move to item c).
c) If solutions a) or b) cannot be used, the order will be determined by a
ballot.
In case one or more run-offs cannot be held due to force majeure, then
precedence will be given to the riders (tied on points) according to
points a), b) or c) of this article.

For any FIME Individual competition in which the last qualifying place or

34 APPENDIX 1 ­ SIDECAR (500cc) TECHNICAL SPECIFICATION
The following technical information was previously detailed in the FIM Technical
Rules. However as 500cc Sidecar's only complete in an FIM European
championship, the rules below were removed from the FIM Technical Rules
(January 2024) and are now contained in this appendix.
However there are some rules which are still contained within the FIM Technical
Rules that will need to be referenced (e.g. capacity, use of titanium, exhaust
systems, control leavers, throttle controls, ignition cut out switches, foot rests,
mudguards etc), together with the information contained in this appendix.
34.1 Weight:
The minimum weight for Track Racing 500cc Sidecars = 115kg
(unladen)
34.2 Wheels, Rims And Tyres For Track Racing Sidecars:
Front Wheel: Minimum rim diameter: 21"
Minimum diameter wheel spindle: 15 mm solid steel shaft permitted only.
Minimum diameter spokes: 3.5 mm M4, steel construction.
Spoke lacing must cross twice.
34.2.1 Rear Wheel:
Min rim diameter: 19". Max rim diameter: 22"
Minimum diameter wheel spindle: 17mm, solid steel shaft permitted
only.
Minimum diameter of spokes: 4mm, M4, steel construction
Spoke lacing must cross twice.
Maximum tyre width:100mm.
A suitable chain guard must be fitted to protect the area where the rear
chain enters onto the real wheel sprocket.
34.2.2 Sidecar Wheel:
Minimum rim diameter: 16"
Minimum diameter wheel spindle: 25 mm tempered steel shaft permitted
only (refer to diagram 1).
Minimum diameter spokes: 4 mm, M4 steel construction.
Spoke lacing must cross twice.
Spindle sleeves must be secured by supplementary self-locking nuts,
e.g. Nylock or castle nuts with split pins.
The minimum width between the wire spoke flanges at the hub must be
75 mm.
The spoke holes must be countersunk on both sides. Plastic hubs re
forbidden.
NOTE: There is no restriction for the size or type of the front or Sidecar
tyres. However, the tyre tread pattern must remain as designed
by the manufacturer.

34.3 Sidecar Construction:
34.3.1 General
For all races, only single cylinder 4-stroke engines are allowed.
The maximum engine capacity is 500cc, four valves maximum fitted with
one carburettor (maximum diameter 38mm) and one spark plug.
The Sidecar must be fixed to the motorcycle in at least four separate
fixing points from the sidecar frame onto the motorcycle chassis (refer
to diagram 2).
Fixing point 1: Must be mounted directly to the rear of the Sidecar
chassis, through the rear wheel plate/arm no more than 150mm in front
of rear wheel spindle must be fixed rigid using a minimum steel bolt
fixing of M10.
Fixing point 2: Diagonal Sidecar support serves as a hand hold for the
passenger, must be fixed rigid to the engine plate using a minimum steel
bolt fixing of M10.
Fixing point 3: Sidecar main tube (centre spar) must be fixed rigid
between the engine plates using a minimum steel bolt fixing of M10
through the nearside and offside engine plate if it is not an integral part
of the chassis.
Fixing point 4: This support bar must be fixed rigid utilising the bottom
front engine bolt.
Fixing points 3 and 4 must be no less than 350mm apart.
NOTE 1: If the motorcycle is fitted with an ACTIVE/MOVING swinging
arm, then an alternative and substantial `Fixing Point 1'
(minimum 8mm thick aluminium or equivalent), must be used
to attach the Sidecar frame to the motorcycle chassis. In this
situation the fixing can be no less than a M8 steel bolt.
NOTE 2: Fixing point 3: this is the main Sidecar tube that runs directly
from the motorcycle chassis to the Sidecar wheel. It is fixed
between the engine plates below the gearbox area and is
secured from both sides through the engine plates using M10
steel bolts (refer to diagram 3).
NOTE 3: Fixing point 4 must be a minimum of 350mm in front of fixing
point 3 .
NOTE 4: All fixings/bolts used for the chassis fixings points (other than
in Note 1 above), must be M10 or larger steel bolts.
Hinged/pivoted Sidecars are strictly forbidden.
The angle of inclination of the motorcycle chassis, with respect to the
vertical line to the ground, must not exceed 15 degrees, and the Sidecar
wheel must not exceed 30 degrees (refer to diagram 5).
The minimum dimensions of a Sidecar available for passenger
accommodation are: minimum length:1000 mm and minimum width: 400
mm.

There must be no movement on any part of the Sidecar fixing points. If
the angle of the sidecar is adjustable (via the positioning of the sidecar
wheel), then it must be clamped in such a way that it is completely
secure using three steel fixing bolts no less than M8 ­ with the addition
of an end stop fitted to the end of the main tube.
The `Toe In and Toe Out' position adjuster (often referred to as the
Sidecar knuckle), must be clamped in such a way that it is completely
secure using two steel fixing bolts no less than M8 (refer to diagram 4)
The fuel tank and oil tank must be securely mounted and positioned
behind the steering head.
The outside Sidecar wheel shall be provided with a protective horizontal
tube (minimum tube diameter: 22 mm), fixed at the same height as the
Sidecar spindle. No component shall protrude beyond this protective
tube.
34.3.2 Drive:
The drive shall be transmitted to the ground only through the rear wheel
of the motorcycle.
34.3.3 Wheel Spindles and Headstock Bolts:
These fixtures must be of a solid construction and NOT drilled or
lightened. They must be fitted with either a self-locking nut or split pin
to.
34.3.4 Rear Wheel and Sidecar Wheel Offset:
The distance between the tracks left by the centre lines of the rear
motorcycle wheel and the Sidecar wheel must be at least 800 mm and
must not exceed 1100 mm.
34.3.5 Wheel Displacement:
To reduce the torque in the steering, a maximum displacement of 75mm
is permitted between the front wheel and the rear wheel.
34.3.6 Handlebars:
Handlebars must be firmly secured to the forks. They must be at a height
above the mid-point of the seat. The motorcycle must have a steering
head which must be fitted like the handlebar and must not be attached
to the unsuspended part of the front wheel suspension.
34.3.7 Front Forks:
The front forks of the motorcycle must be securely fitted to the headstock
using a headstock spindle of minimum 14 mm diameter.
34.3.8 Steering:
The steering of the front wheel must be via `leading link' front forks, with
the wheel supported equally each side (long or short leading link type).
All other types of front wheel steering and suspension are forbidden.

34.3.9 Engine Position:
The position of the engine is optional, except that it must be positioned
in front of the rear wheel. The centre line of the engine corresponds to
the crankshaft axis (for longitudinal engines) and to the centre of line of
the cylinder block (for transverse engines)
The centre line of the engine shall not exceed more than 160 mm
beyond the centre line of the rear wheel of the motorcycle.
If the engine and gearbox are mounted on plates, the minimum
thickness of the albased alloy engine plates must be 8 mm.
34.3.10 Exhaust System:
The exhaust system must not extend beyond the outer circumference
of the rear tyre and must not end before the centre of the rear wheel
spindle.
The motorcycle exhaust system must be securely fixed at four points ­
the point of fixture at the cylinder head is considered as one point.
The silencer must be fitted to the frame with at least one fixing (refer to
diagram 5).
Additionally a second flexible fixing must be fitted from the first third of
the silencer to the frame. This second flexible fixing should be a steel
cable of at least 3mm diameter or a strong steel spring.
The silencer must be of a type that is currently listed within the FIM
Technical rules.
The silencer must be fitted with a suitable heat shield.
34.3.11 Carburettor:
The diameter of the motorcycle's carburettor will be a maximum of 38
mm (refer to diagram 6).
34.3.12 Riders Seat Height:
The minimum height of the rider's seat shall be 400mm, measured from
the ground of the motorcycle in a fully laden (e.g. with rider and
passenger) position.
34.3.13 Ground Clearance:
The minimum ground clearance at the front edge of the Sidecar platform
is 150 mm. The ground clearance of a fully laden Sidecar (with rider and
passenger) must not be less than 100 mm at any point.
34.3.14 Wheel Protection:
The rear wheel and Sidecar wheel must be covered or protected with a
solid material, ensuring that any fixings do not become loose or come
apart during racing.
